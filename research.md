## Work in Progress

**The Role of Lender Heterogeneity and Machine Learning in Mortgage Lending**

**Abstract**: 
This paper examines whether the distributional effects of machine learning-based mortgage pricing differ between depository banks and non-depository (”shadow bank lenders. Building on the framework of Fuster et al. (2022), I use post-2018 Home Mortgage Disclosure Act (HMDA) data to estimate three models of increasing sophistication, OLS, LASSO, and Random Forest, predicting the rate spread on originated mortgages, separately for bank and nonbank lenders. I find that the move from traditional linear prediction to machine learning generates measurably unequal effects across racial groups, with Black borrowers disproportionately less likely to benefit from the technology shift relative to Asian and White borrowers. While the average racial gap in predicted rate change is similar at banks and nonbanks, the structure of the inequality differs sharply across lender types. At nonbank lenders, the racial gap, when comparing Asian borrowers to Black borrowers, is concentrated near the median of the distribution and dissipates in the tails. At bank lenders, this gap is increasing across the distribution, reaching its maximum among borrowers who would already face the largest rate increases under ML. These findings suggest that the distributional consequences of algorithmic underwriting depend not only on the technology itself but also on the institutional context in which it is deployed, with implications for fair lending policy in an era of expanding nonbank market share.
